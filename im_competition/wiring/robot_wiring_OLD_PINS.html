The robot as it is now: Pico, 6 optocouplers, L298N, two 12 V encoder motors (swapped sides), two line IR sensors, the right ultrasonic and the drop servo, running from batteries only.

Before you switch on

  1. Nothing on VBUS (pin 40). VBUS only has power while the USB cable is in. Encoders or IRs fed from it work on the laptop and go dead on battery, and the speed control then runs the motors flat out. Feed them from 3V3 (pin 36).
  2. AA pack + to VSYS (pin 39), − to a Pico GND. Measure VSYS on battery: 3.6–5.5 V is right. 4 cells (about 6 V) is too high.
  3. Check the encoder is separate from the motor. With a multimeter on continuity, touch the encoder plug's GND pin and each motor tab. It must not beep. If it beeps, don't connect that encoder: its GND would join the two sides.
  4. Encoder VCC on 3.3 V only. The encoder outputs follow its supply, and 5 V on C1/C2 can damage the Pico.
  5. ECHO through its divider. 1 kΩ in series, 2 kΩ from GP17 to GND.
  6. Pico GND and L298N GND never meet. Check with the meter: no beep between the two left-board GND rails.
  7. L298N jumpers: ENA and ENB jumpers removed (the optos drive those pins), 5V-EN jumper left on (it powers the pull-ups).
  8. Keep encoder wires away from motor wires. If counts jump while the motors run, twist each encoder's four wires together and add a 100 nF capacitor across each motor's tabs.

Battery check sketch

Upload it, unplug the USB and run on battery. Motors stay off. It shows whether both encoders still work without the laptop.

  1. Upload, then unplug the USB and switch on the AA pack.
  2. Turn each wheel slowly by hand. The Pico's green LED should flicker for both wheels.
  3. Dark for one wheel: that encoder has no power or no ground on battery. Its VCC goes to 3V3 (pin 36), its GND to a Pico GND.
  4. Hold each IR over white and black: the LED on the IR module itself must switch.

  


  

    

      
What the encoders fix

    

    

      
Driving straight. Your 0 / 72 PWM calibration only holds for one battery level and one floor. With counts, the code can speed up or slow down the right motor until both wheels have turned the same amount, so the robot goes straight on its own.

      
Turning by angle. The 10° search turn was set by time and kept missing. Spinning on the spot, each wheel travels (wheel spacing × π × angle ÷ 360), so a turn can stop at a count instead of a time. The same counts give the distances for the run to T1.
