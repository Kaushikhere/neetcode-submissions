    A 90-second run

    A time budget for the whole run, drawn to scale. The robot must shut itself down at 90 s, so the plan stops at 88 s and keeps 10 s spare.

      Input for the build

      How each stage can work with your robot as it is now, and what to add with the Rs 500.

      Find yourself at the start

      The start box has 30 cm walls on three sides: behind you and up both sides to about y = 58. The fourth side is open up-field.

      • Spin slowly through 360° and log the right ultrasonic distance against heading. Each wall shows up as a dip; the bottom of a dip is the square-on distance to that wall.
      • The open side gives long readings or no echo. That direction is up-field (heading 90°).
      • Back-wall dip gives y. The two side-wall dips each give x (from the left and from the right); average them, and treat a big mismatch as a bad sweep.
      • Add the distance from the sensor face to the robot centre to every reading.
      • Measuring the turn angle by time did not work well on your 10° search. A gyro (MPU6050) makes this sweep, and every later turn, reliable.

      Drop at T1

      • Put the drop chute on the middle of the wheel axle. The robot turns about that point, so the drop spot does not move when the heading is slightly off, and all the maths uses one point.
      • Drop something flat that will not roll, such as a large washer or a coin stack with felt underneath. Its centre is unambiguous. Avoid balls.
      • Turn to the bearing of T1, then drive the distance with the gyro holding the heading. The table on the left gives the turn and distance from each marked pose.
      • One SG90 servo can release the item and later swing the pointer out.

      From T1 to the pocket

      • On the practice board the line circles T1 about 30–40 cm out. After the drop, face right (heading 0°) and drive about 39 cm until the line sensors see black, then turn left and follow it up the field.
      • Going round the loop the other way ends at the dead end (B). If you reach it, turn round.
      • The passage between the two walls is about 29 cm centre to centre, so roughly 27 cm clear for a robot that must fit a 25 cm box. Follow the line slowly here.
      • Just past the passage the line forks (D). The right branch goes over the T-shaped wall and stops. Keep left.
      • The hairpin (E) is a turn of about 140° to the left. Slow down; if the line is lost, pivot left on the spot until it is found again.
      • Treat black seen by an outer line sensor near the board edge as the border, not the line, and stop.

      The last 25 cm to T2

      • The line ends at the open mouth of a pocket between two rails, about 40 cm wide and 26 cm deep. T2 is estimated against the middle of the rung at the back (rung C2).
      • At the line end, turn right about 40° to face into the pocket (heading about −63°).
      • Drive in using your current wall-follow code: the right ultrasonic sees the left rail (W1). For a 25 cm robot, hold about 7–8 cm from the side to stay centred.
      • Stop when the front sensor reads the rung at the pointer's length, then unfold the pointer. The pointer must stay on the T2 side of the rung, so do not poke over it.

      Competition II: the speed race

      • Your right-side wall follower is already most of the code for the circular track. With the sensor on the right, drive anticlockwise so the outer wall stays on your right. If the organisers fix the direction the other way, move the sensor to the left side.
      • Add a fixed turn so the robot curves by itself and the sensor only trims: inner wheel speed ÷ outer = (R − b/2) ÷ (R + b/2). With the robot centre about R = 105 cm from the middle and wheels b = 18 cm apart, the inner wheel runs about 84% of the outer.
      • The wall is smooth and slippery, which invites a mechanical guide: two small rollers on the right side touching the wall, with the robot steered slightly into it, can run flat out with no sensing at all.

      Pins on the Pico

      • Full wiring, breadboard layout and pin map: IM Robot Wiring.
      • Used: GP10–GP15 motors through the optocouplers, GP16/17 right ultrasonic, GP18–GP21 encoders, GP22/GP28 front ultrasonic, GP26/GP27 IR.
      • Spare for the next parts: gyro on GP4 (SDA) and GP5 (SCL), start button GP2, servo GP3 (wired directly, not through the optocouplers), line sensors GP6–GP8.
      • Both ultrasonic ECHO pins go through a 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ divider: ECHO outputs 5 V and the Pico's pins take 3.3 V.
      • Start a 90 s timer when the run starts and call stopMotors() forever once it passes 88 s.

      Check before you trust the map

      • Everything here comes from one angled photo, so positions are good to about 5 cm, and the far walls are the least certain. Tape-measure T1, rung C2 and the passage before tuning numbers.
      • T2 is not marked on the board. Its position comes from Figure 1 of the rules (against the middle of the rung, inside the pocket) and from the clip on that rung. Ask the organisers to confirm it.
      • The competition board gets new decoy lines, and the start region's top edge (dashed, assumed at y = 55) is not drawn on the practice board.
      • The start-pose headings assume the stem of each yellow T is the robot's forward direction.
      • Check your robot, wires included, fits through 27 cm and inside a 25 cm box with the pointer folded.
